The Sequential Rise of Political Animosity in Economically Declining and Prospering Places: Evidence from 3 Billion Geo-located Tweets ↗

Sun Young Park

Analyzing three billion geolocated tweets from the U.S., France, and South Korea (2013–2022), Park finds that political animosity was higher in economically declining places before populist breakthroughs and was associated with subsequent populist gains. Animosity then rose in prospering places as populist victories and rhetoric provoked perceived threats, suggesting that economic discontent and political mobilization fuel hostility in sequence.

The Measurement Revolution? Credible Measurement and Inference in the Age of AI ↗

Melissa Dell & Ashesh Rambachan

Dell and Rambachan argue that AI is making once-prohibitive measurement from text, images, audio, and video cheap enough to perform at scale. That abundance shifts the core problem from finding any workable proxy to choosing among many plausible measures that can produce different empirical conclusions. They organize AI measurement into discovery, construct definition, and observation, emphasizing explicit criteria and validation at each stage. For credible inference, they favor representative validation samples that reveal and correct model errors; when those samples are unavailable, researchers must state and defend stronger assumptions about how the measurements travel across settings.
